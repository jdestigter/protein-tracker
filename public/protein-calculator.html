What the research says

Protein recommendations vary by source because they answer different questions — "how much to avoid deficiency" versus "how much to build or protect muscle."

SourceRecommendationFor whom
RDA (US Dietary Reference Intake)0.8 g/kg (0.36 g/lb)Minimum to prevent deficiency in sedentary adults
ACSM / Academy of Nutrition & Dietetics1.2–2.0 g/kg (0.54–0.9 g/lb)Active adults and athletes
ISSN position stand (Jäger et al., 2017)1.4–2.0 g/kg (0.64–0.9 g/lb)Building and maintaining muscle
Mettler et al., 2010~2.3 g/kg (~1.0 g/lb)Preserving lean mass in a calorie deficit

The pattern: the more active you are and the harder you're dieting, the more protein it takes to keep the muscle you have. That's what the activity and goal adjustments above reflect. Full citations are on our sources page.

Frequently asked questions

How much protein do I need per day?

It depends on your weight, activity, and goal. The RDA (0.8 g/kg) is a floor, not a target — it's the amount that prevents deficiency, not the amount that supports training or dieting. For most active people, 0.7–1.0 g per pound of body weight per day is the practical range, which is what this calculator lands in.

Do I need more protein while losing weight?

Yes. In a deficit, your body will burn muscle as well as fat unless you give it a reason not to. Higher protein plus resistance training is that reason — Mettler et al. found ~2.3 g/kg preserved lean mass during weight loss far better than 1.0 g/kg. This calculator's "lose weight" goal uses the highest multiplier for exactly that reason.

How much protein on a GLP-1 medication (Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound)?

More important than ever. In the STEP 1 trial's body-composition substudy, roughly 40% of weight lost was lean mass. GLP-1s suppress appetite, so it's easy to under-eat protein without noticing. Clinicians commonly suggest 1.2–1.5 g/kg per day plus resistance training — and tracking daily is the only way to know you're hitting it.

Can I eat too much protein?

For healthy people, intakes well above the RDA haven't been shown to harm kidney function. If you have kidney disease or another medical condition, check with your doctor before increasing protein.

Does timing matter, or just the daily total?

Daily total first. Spreading protein across 3–4 meals of 25–40 g each may squeeze out a little extra muscle protein synthesis, but no timing scheme fixes a day that's 50 g short. Hit the number, then worry about distribution.
