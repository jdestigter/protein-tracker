Privacy Policy

Last updated: March 23, 2026

DailyP ("we", "our", or "the app") is committed to protecting your privacy. This policy explains what data DailyP collects, how it's used, and your rights.

Data We Collect

Food log entries: When you log food via voice or text, the text of what you said or typed is sent to our server to calculate protein content. We do not store this text on our servers after processing — it is used only to return the protein estimate and then discarded.

Local data: Your protein goals, daily food logs, and history are stored locally on your device. This data never leaves your device except when food text is sent for processing as described above.

Voice data: When you use voice input, speech recognition is performed by Apple's on-device speech recognition system. Audio is not sent to DailyP's servers.

Data We Don't Collect

We do not collect, store, or have access to: your name, email address, location, health data, contacts, photos, browsing history, device identifiers, or any other personal information. We do not require an account to use DailyP.

Third-Party Services

Food text input is processed using Anthropic's Claude API to estimate protein content. Anthropic's privacy policy can be found at anthropic.com/privacy. No personally identifiable information is included in these API requests.

Analytics & Advertising

DailyP does not include any analytics, tracking, or advertising SDKs. We do not track how you use the app. We do not show ads.

Data Retention

All food log data is stored locally on your device. If you delete the app, all data is permanently deleted. We have no ability to recover your data because we never had access to it.

Children's Privacy

DailyP is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect any information from children.

Changes to This Policy

We may update this policy from time to time. Any changes will be posted on this page with an updated date.

Contact

If you have questions about this privacy policy, contact us at privacy@dailyp.app.
